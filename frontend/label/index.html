Headline labeling

Labeling guide

About the region? Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, or Poland — not just a passing mention. No skips the rest. Unsure is saved and reviewed later; it is not scored against the model.

Uses pretext language? Loaded or emotional words, blame, accusation, or a threat. No means every frame is absent. Yes opens the frames.

  • Persecuted Russians — Russians or Russian speakers treated as victims, including “compatriots”.
  • Nazism — Nazis, fascists, or glorifying them.
  • Illegitimate borders — the state or its borders should not exist; “historical Russian lands”.
  • NATO aggression — NATO or the West preparing an attack, or militarizing near Russia.
  • Russophobia — the state is hostile or discriminating.
  • Military threats — Russia may respond or retaliate with force.

Judge the wording, not whether the claim is true. Neutral fact reporting is absent. A quote from a Russian official counts. Mild = a hint or a loaded word. Strong = central. Take a break every 15–20 headlines.

Keys: Y/N region, U unsure, P pretext yes, 1–6 toggle that frame, Enter next, B back. Shortcuts pause while the note box is focused, except Enter.

Ask yourself: Is this headline just telling me what happened, or telling me how to feel about that country?
