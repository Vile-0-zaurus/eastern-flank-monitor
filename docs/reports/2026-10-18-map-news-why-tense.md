# Map, news, and why tense

Written 2026-10-18. Plain words. No scores were changed. No tags were moved. Nothing was made public.

1. The main screen now has five country cards, a map, a news list, and a week slider. Play moves the week, and the cards, map, and news move with it.
2. Calm is shown as Normal. Watchful and Elevated are shown as Tense. A proposed Serious or Critical alarm is shown as "Danger signs, unconfirmed."
3. The map is stored in the project. It does not load pictures from the internet. A dot stays for four weeks. Click a dot for what happened, the date, and the source.
4. The news list shows Russian state-media headlines for the selected week. Each line has the original text, an English line when one was stored, the outlet, the date, and a source link. If no English line was stored, the screen says so in plain words. It does not invent a translation.
5. Click a country or a dot to open Why tense. It has three blocks: what happened, what official sources say, and what Russian state media says.
6. Official lines were copied only from pages that showed a date in that week. There are 18 lines across 15 weeks, from Finland, Estonia, Lithuania, Poland, NATO, and the US State Department. Each line says it was summarized by AI from the linked source. A week with no collected page says no source was found.
7. Two US pages would not load on the live site. Those two lines use Internet Archive copies of the State Department pages, and the date is the date printed on the page. The Zapad week has no official page in this set, so that week says no source was found.
8. The self-test passed. It checks every week, checks that map dots match the event file, checks that links are real web links, and checks for clipping, raw country codes, and NaN.
